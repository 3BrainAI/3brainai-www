Lausitz, Germany — post-mining transformation

Environmentally impacted former open-pit lignite mine undergoing large-scale flooding and redevelopment.

WATCH

Assessment summary

Dated optical observations show a substantial change from predominantly exposed surfaces in June 2019 to a large water body in May 2026. The synthetic milestone is directionally supported. Engineering completion, water quality and geotechnical condition cannot be determined from imagery alone.

Observed vs Declared

Declared

Flooding and post-mining transformation substantially advanced within the monitored area by May 2026.

Observed

The May 2026 observation shows a large water body where the June 2019 baseline showed predominantly exposed surfaces.

Findings

  • Substantial visible land and water-body change is present.
  • The dated pair provides comparable surface context.
  • The visible change supports the direction of the synthetic milestone, not overall completion.

Uncertainty

Different acquisition dates and seasonal surface conditions affect direct comparison. The imagery does not establish cause, engineering completion, water quality, geotechnical safety or regulatory compliance.

Recommended next step

Reconcile the observation pair with the synthetic milestone record and supporting engineering documentation in human review.

Confidence applies only to the visible land and water-body observation — not to overall project completion, safety or compliance.

Evidence Pack sample — synthetic public-safe data

Evidence Pack

German North Sea — offshore wind development monitoring

Renewable-energy development area with regularly spaced surface-visible offshore structures.

WATCH

Assessment summary

Repeat-pass radar observations provide a dated surface record for reconciling a synthetic offshore wind installation milestone. Surface-visible point targets are present, but the available evidence cannot confirm subsea works, contractual completion or the identity and condition of individual assets.

Observed vs Declared

Declared

Offshore wind installation activity substantially progressed across the monitored area during June 2026.

Observed

Both repeat-pass radar observations show multiple surface-visible point targets. Differences in brightness and texture do not by themselves confirm installation progress or asset condition.

Findings

  • The repeat-pass acquisitions use comparable mode, orbit and geometry.
  • Multiple bright point targets are visible at the sea surface.
  • The surface record is insufficient to verify subsea or contractual completion.

Uncertainty

Sea state, speckle, acquisition geometry and radar response affect interpretation. Sentinel-1 GRD VV contains backscatter, not interferometric phase, and cannot confirm subsea works, deformation or structural condition.

Recommended next step

Reconcile the radar pair with the synthetic milestone record, operator logs and supporting engineering documentation in human review.

Confidence applies only to the dated surface-visible context — not to installation progress, asset identity or condition.

Evidence gap

More data does not automatically create decision-ready evidence.

Physical-asset monitoring often combines imagery, schedules, reports and expert judgement without a consistent way to express uncertainty, provenance or review state.

Between formal review points

A governed signal for human review.

A lender financing a physical-asset project receives a versioned Evidence Pack comparing declared milestone progress with the available observation. A WATCH status routes the evidence to human review; it does not trigger a credit decision.

How it works

From inputs to a versioned Evidence Pack

The workflow structures evidence for accountable human review. It does not publish or decide automatically.

  1. 01 Physical and project inputs
  2. 02 Evidence validation
  3. 03 Structured findings and uncertainty
  4. 04 Human review
  5. 05 Versioned Evidence Pack

Validation path

Validate the workflow before operational use.

Evidence Readiness is a preliminary check of whether inputs and review conditions are sufficient to run the workflow. A shadow-mode pilot then reviews Evidence Packs in parallel with existing controls — without replacing live decision processes. Progress depends on explicit validation gates.

Secondary architecture

Governance Layer behind the product

The Trusted Data Plane / Governance Layer supports CRI through provenance, review state, versioning, audit trail and controlled publication.

  • Provenance Source chain and input conditions remain attached to outputs.
  • Review state Human review status stays visible wherever a status appears.
  • Versioning Outputs remain reconstructable across releases.
  • Audit trail Evidence and review history remain traceable across versions.
  • Controlled publication Publication remains a governed step, not an automatic release.
