Why institutional evidence is the investment thesis

The investment story

From AI capability
to evidence an institution can use.

The opportunity we are pursuing is a domain-specific verification workflow around model capability.

01 / Why now

More AI output raises a harder question.

What can an institution rely on, and how will a reviewer defend it later? We see an opportunity in the work between a generated answer and an accepted institutional record.

02 / Where the domain matters

A model does not supply the whole mandate.

General-purpose models and their providers can support evaluation. A bank-specific workflow still needs the authoritative project scope, usable source rights, technical competence and a named person who can accept or reject the result.

03 / Why this starting point

Physical assets make the boundary concrete.

A dated image, a contractor declaration and an adviser’s response answer different questions. CRI concentrates on making those differences usable in construction finance and collateral review.

Demand, competitive advantage and willingness to pay remain to be tested through bank engagement and contracted pilots.

A problem seen from the owner’s side

Delivery responsibility came before the software.

Under shareholder mandates in industrial and energy investments, Dusan Prikryl worked on owner-side crisis and CAPEX transformation and coordinated major European technology suppliers. The practical question was direct: what is actually delivered, what supports the report and who is accountable?

He later added digital product development, data operations, analytics and AI. CRI brings those two layers into a bank-focused evidence workflow: physical-asset judgement translated into traceable records and explicit review boundaries.

This combination shapes the product’s starting point. Institution-specific validation must show how well it works for bank reviewers.
